# The Ass Channel — master plan

English comedy-biology YouTube. Premise: "every type of ass, explained" as a
numbered listicle, in the style of Sam O'Nella meets TierZoo. Cartoon
illustrations, deadpan TTS narration, one weird butt per chapter.

## The niche

Reference points:

- Sam O'Nella Academy: stick figures, dark/deadpan history comedy, ~4.8M subs.
  Proved: voice + script beats production quality. Rough visuals are identity,
  not weakness.
- TierZoo: biology facts as tier lists. Massive, fully monetized, educational.
- Gross Science (NOVA): "sea cucumber butts" is literally an episode.

Our lane: listicle documentary + cartoon + dry humor. "Animal butts explained"
is unsaturated as a dedicated format.

## The pun strategy (monetization-safe)

"Ass" has two meanings. We use both but never in metadata:

- TITLE/THUMBNAIL: never the word "ass" alone. Safe variants: "butts",
  "rear ends", "donkeys", or "asses" ONLY inside "All Types of Asses" where the
  thumbnail literally shows donkeys (the classifier reads it as the animal).
- SCRIPT: "ass" allowed freely — it means donkey. Every time we say "ass" we
  can legally mean Equus africanus asinus. The joke is the double meaning.
- YouTube policy (advertiser-friendly guidelines, checked 2026-11): educational
  anatomy/biology content earns revenue; profanity in title/thumb = limited;
  sexualized content = none. Cartoon animal butts = clean.

## Writing rules (the not-AI, not-boring part)

The enemy sounds like this:

> "The wombat possesses a remarkable adaptation in the form of a reinforced
> posterior, which serves a dual purpose of protection and burrow excavation.
> Furthermore, this marsupial produces cube-shaped fecal matter."

That is a Wikipedia citation wearing a costume. Nobody talks like that.

The fix, from the style refs:

1. **Write for the mouth, not the page.** If you can't say it in one breath
   without a comma, split it. 8-15 words per sentence. Read every line aloud.
   Target ~190 wpm like the Oday videos — brisk but not rushed.

2. **Every fact gets a reaction.** Facts are free. The reaction is the product.
   The narrator isn't a teacher, he's a guy who can't believe this either.
   BAD: "Wombats produce cube-shaped feces."
   GOOD: "Wombats poop in cubes. Actual cubes. Like someone cut their poop
   with a tiny ruler."

3. **The "yeah, and" joke structure.** State fact, then escalate it one more
   notch. "Sea cucumbers breathe through their butt. They also eat through
   it. This animal has one hole and refuses to specialize."

4. **Name the animal's name like it's a person.** "Kevin is a wombat. Kevin
   has a problem: everything wants to eat him. Kevin's solution is a butt
   so hard it cracks skulls."

5. **Questions are the engine.** Your Oday transcripts ask constantly. Keep
   that: "But why a cube? Why not a sphere like everyone else?" Then answer.

6. **The turn.** Your style has one. Here: the reveal that the best ass
   belongs to the animal you least expect. Build a fake ranking, then
   upset it at the end.

7. **Zero throat-clearing.** No "welcome back", no "in this video we will",
   no "did you know that". First sentence IS the fact. Your Oday transcripts
   do this — "The first situation: a single verse" — same instinct, joke
   instead of verse.

8. **BANNED in the script** (auto-check these): "remarkable", "fascinating",
   "incredible", "amazing", "astonishing", "did you know", "little-known",
   "in fact", "it turns out", "believe it or not", "imagine a world",
   "what if I told you", "without further ado", "let's dive in",
   "journey", "delve", "unleash", "unlock", "elevate", "showcase".

   Also banned: em dashes, three-part lists that sound like TED talks,
   sentences longer than 20 words, the word "posterior" used unironically
   (it's allowed exactly once, for the joke).

9. **Allowed vocabulary:** butt, ass, rear, behind, backside, bum, rump,
   caboose, dump truck. Rotate naturally. "Rectum" for the deadpan
   science moment exactly once.

10. **Sign-off like Oday's**: call back to the hook, CTA that fits the
    topic, two next-episode options, end cleanly.

## The scene/visual spec

Stick figures + flat 2D cartoon, same DNA as the Oday videos but comedy:

- Backgrounds: flat color fields per chapter (savanna tan, burrow brown,
  ocean blue, forest green — one hue per animal)
- Animals: simplified geometric cartoons, always shown BUTT-FIRST when
  the narration is about the rear. That's the visual joke.
- The mascot narrator (hoodie guy) returns, points at butts with a stick.
- Chapter cards: big number + animal name + a rear-view silhouette.
- Numbers big and yellow-boxed (borrow the infographic grammar from
  the old project — it still works for stats inside cartoons).
- Text on screen: minimal, speech bubbles only, plus the scientific name
  in small italic like a museum label (that's the recurring gag —
  the video pretends to be serious science while showing cartoon butts).
- The Prophet-seal trick: when we hit the donkey chapters, a golden
  badge marks "LITERAL ASS" vs "FIGURATIVE ASS" — a running visual
  tracker of which meaning is on screen.

## The pipeline (all on tlab, all free)

```
job folder: ~/oday/factory/jobs/YYYY-MM-DD-asses/
  01-brief.md          topic + angle picked by human
  02-facts.md          researched facts + sources, I verify each one
  03-script.md         narration script with [SCENE] markers
  04-check.sh          lint: banned words, sentence length, wpm estimate
  05-voice.wav         Kokoro ONNX or edge-tts (free, CPU) English male
  06-beats.json        whisper-timestamped sentence beats
  07-images/           pollinations.ai flat illustrations, locked prompt
  08-build/            Remotion scenes (reuse existing renderer) OR
                       ffmpeg Ken Burns concat for v1
  09-final.mp4
  10-thumbs/           3 thumbnail variants
```

Stage-by-stage:

1. **Facts.** I research each animal, pull 3 verified facts + source links.
   You approve the fact sheet. No invented biology.
2. **Script.** I write ~2,800 words using rules above. Self-check via
   lint script (banned words, avg sentence length, joke-per-minute floor).
3. **Voice.** Kokoro-ONNX `am_adam` or `bm_george` on CPU, ~190wpm.
   Free, local, good enough for deadpan. Edge-tts `en-US-GuyNeural` as
   fallback (free but hits Microsoft servers).
4. **Timestamps.** Whisper.cpp on CPU, sentence-level beats -> beats.json.
   ~2min processing for 15min audio on 8 cores.
5. **Images.** Pollinations.ai `flux` model, free, one illustration per
   beat (~60-80 images for 15min). Locked prompt prefix = consistent
   style. 1792x1024.
6. **Assemble.** v1: ffmpeg Ken Burns on each image + concat by beats +
   SRT subtitles + loudnorm -14 LUFS. Fast, boring, works.
   v2: port the visuals.json Remotion renderer from infographics_Videos
   and add cartoon scene templates (chapter cards, rank reveal, the
   "LITERAL/FIGURATIVE ASS" tracker badge).
7. **QC.** Contact sheet of every 30s, check frozen frames, text
   overflow, loudness meter, thumbnail legibility at 120px.
8. **Package.** 3 titles, 3 thumbnails, description with chapter
   timestamps + a straight-faced "sources" section (National
   Geographic, Discover Wildlife, journals).
