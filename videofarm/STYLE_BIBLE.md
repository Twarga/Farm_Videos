## STYLE 1 — "Professor" (EverythingProfessor format)

### Format
- Title formula: `Every <X> & <Their Effects|How To …|What They Actually …> Explained [in N Minutes]`,
  `Simply Explaining <…> EVERYONE <…>`, `<…> YOU Might Have Without Knowing`. One ALL-CAPS word for emphasis.
- A **list video**: 12–22 items ("chapters"), one per item, roughly equal length (35–60 s each).
- Length 12–20 min (sweet spot for mid-roll ads). 2,000–3,000 spoken words at ~160 wpm.
- YouTube chapters = the items (`12 chapters  Kratom | Nitrous Oxide | DXM …`).
- Thumbnail = the roster: big black title with one red word, grid of 8–10 colored circles, each holding one icon,
  label under each circle. The video re-uses this roster as its chapter-transition device.

### Script (measured from transcripts)
- **No intro, no "welcome", no hook paragraph.** First word of the video = name of item #1.
- Every item follows the same skeleton:
  1. Item name, alone ("Depression.")
  2. Definition sentence that repeats the name + a mechanism ("…is a mental disorder that drains the color out of life.
     It affects how your brain produces serotonin and dopamine…")
  3. **Second-person lived experience** — "You wake up exhausted… Food tastes like nothing…" (concrete, sensory, 4–6 beats)
  4. **Myth-bust** — "People say *just think positive*. But…" / "People think ADHD means…, but that's wrong."
  5. Escalation / long-term consequence — "Over time…"
  6. **Quotable closing line** — "It whispers quietly until you forget what feeling alive used to be like."
- Finance variant opens items with a **mini-story** ("Picture this. You just had a hard month flipping burgers…
  your bank account only gets $1,456") then explains, with jokes ("makes sure your taco truck isn't infested with rats").
- Sentences short; one image per sentence. Humor is dry, slightly dark. Never hedges.

### Visuals (frame grids f1/grid_a, grid_b, grid_fin)
- **World:** solid saturated mustard-yellow background (#F7C325 / #F5B800). Optional floor strip (grey concrete,
  wood planks, tiles) or a minimal room set (couch, lamp, bookshelf, plant) — never a full background painting.
- **Character:** one recurring "everyman": huge round bald head, pale/white skin, two big round eyes with small pupils,
  tiny mouth, 2–3 hair tufts, small ears, simple body. **Outfit changes per chapter** (grey hoodie for depression,
  maroon tee for anxiety, green tee for panic, McDonald's uniform for the burger-job story). Expressions do the acting.
- Line art: clean black outlines (thin in older videos, thick in newer), flat fills, soft cell shading, light texture.
  Newer videos = AI-image look (thick outlines, vivid flat colors, occasional garbled sign text).
- **Persistent chapter label**: item name in bold black ALL-CAPS sans, top-center, on screen for the whole chapter.
- **Text-only beat cards**: yellow screen with a single phrase centered ("BUT", "OH BOY", "THEN THE CRASH COMES",
  "BUT IN REALITY") or a huge condensed word ("FRACTIONAL"). Used for punchlines and turns.
- Small labels on objects (thought-bubble words "STORY / REASON / CAUSE", sign "RISKY", "CAN'T TOUCH 'TIL 65").
- Visual metaphors literalized: depression shadow behind the character, brain in thought bubble, roller coaster
  for bipolar, doors labeled SOCIAL / WORK / LIFE, dragon guarding a vault.
- **Chapter transition**: white screen, roster circles (thumbnail grid) slide/zoom to the next item's circle + label.
- **No burned-in subtitles.** Only the chapter label and in-scene words.
- Motion is minimal: hard cuts, slow push-ins/zooms, occasional element pop. Shot length 2.5–4 s
  (finance video measured 2.6 s avg cut).
- Audio: calm male narrator, light background music, sparse pops/whooshes.

## STYLE 2 — "Stickman" (ThatGuy / Productive Peter / Clarity Animations)

### Format
- Niche: self-improvement, psychology, productivity, social skills, money habits. Shorts AND 8–15 min long-form.
- Titles: imperative, confrontational — "stop being BORING", "STOP DOING THIS…", "HE DOESN'T CARE", "You Sound WEAK".
- Thumbnails: black background, white stick figure, neon red/green accents, one shouted phrase.

### Script
- Second person, conversational, punchy; rhetorical openers ("Raise your hand if you've ever said 'I don't have time'").
- Pattern: relatable failure → reframe ("Discipline isn't one big heroic act. It's one small decision repeated.") →
  concrete micro-actions in parallel structure ("You don't need to run 5 miles. You need to put your shoes on.") →
  short punch line → callback.
- Very short lines, lots of fragments, deliberate pauses. ~150 wpm. Shorts ≈ 170–200 words/min of video.
- Their pipeline: 1 locked character reference image → a "visual flow" (sequence of actions) per chapter →
  multi-shot image-to-video clips (Veo 3.1 / Seedance 2.0, ~8–15 s each) → voice in 40–50-word chunks →
  CapCut: music at −15/−20 dB, voice up, optional old-TV monochrome effect, export.

### Visuals (f2/grid_a)
- Black ink line art on **cream/off-white paper**, heavy vignette, slight old-TV/film grain; mostly monochrome,
  rare accent color (red alarm, green success).
- Character: perfect circle head, two **dot eyes**, one-stroke mouth/eyebrows, stick limbs, thick uniform stroke.
  Often oversized head in close-ups.
- Backgrounds: fairly detailed line-art sets (bedroom, desk + laptop, park bench + tree, street with cars),
  same ink weight, no fill or flat white fill.
- Shot grammar: alternates wide establishing → extreme close-up on the face for the emotion beat → prop insert
  (phone reading "21 HOURS"). Cuts every 2–3 s. Motion = character actions (walk, fall into bed, brush teeth), shakes.
- Captions: optional; when used, one big centered word at a time (white, black stroke). Many channels skip them.

## Why our first long-form ("ailabs") failed against these references
- No character → no acting; every shot was an infographic card. Both reference styles are **character-first**:
  each sentence is acted out by "you".
- Too much small text per frame (cards, sources, sub-labels). References show ONE idea, ONE big subject per shot.
- Shots held too long with fiddly animation; references hard-cut every 2.5–4 s.
- Hand-written bespoke scene code per video → only a strong model can make it, and quality varies per agent.

## Implication for the farm
- Production unit = **shot** (one sentence ≈ one shot, 2.5–4 s), not a hand-coded scene.
- Each style = a fixed **style pack**: palette, character model, set library, transition device, caption rule,
  script template. An AI only fills a constrained JSON storyboard (`shot: {line, character pose/expression/outfit,
  set, props, text card, camera}`); the renderer is fixed code → small models can drive it.
