1 · Where does your money go?

42s — You deposit $100. It's not in a vault. Banks keep a slice and lend the rest — and when the Fed cut the slice to 0%, the lending didn't stop.

2 · What is money, anyway?

44s — Paper money is just paper. Most "money" is numbers in a bank's ledger, created when banks make loans. Money is a shared promise.

3 · Why can't we just print more money?

43s — Prices aren't fixed. Zimbabwe printed a $100-trillion note in 2009, then quietly abandoned its own currency.

4 · What actually happened in 2008?

44s — Banks ground bad mortgages into "safe" packages. When homeowners stopped paying, Lehman fell and taxpayers paid $700B.

5 · The most powerful force in finance is boring

47s — $100 a month at 7% for 40 years is $262,000. Your money makes babies, and the babies make babies. Time is the ingredient.

6 · One number runs your life

44s — A credit score is just a prediction: will you pay back? Payment history is 35% of it — and the score exists for the lender, not you.

7 · How insurers get rich

44s — Premiums pool risk, but the real profit machine is investing the pool while it sits there — what Buffett calls "float".

8 · Where do your taxes actually go?

47s — Slice a federal tax dollar: Social Security, health, defense — and a 13¢ strip called interest that just overtook the Pentagon.

9 · A stock is just a tiny slice

48s — Not a lottery ticket: a share is 1% of a real shop's ovens, debt, and profits. Price swings daily; the business earns slowly.

10 · One committee, all your prices

49s — 12 unelected people set one number — the overnight bank rate. In 2022–23 they cranked it 0% → 5.5%, fastest in 40 years.
