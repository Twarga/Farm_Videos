## The short answer

A VPS is a virtual machine you rent from a hosting provider. You get an operating system, a share of a physical server's resources, and enough control to run your own services.

## What you pay for

Compare the effective monthly price, not only the advertised number. Check RAM, CPU, disk, transfer, server location, renewal price, billing term, and whether IPv4 costs extra. The catalog puts those facts next to the catch so you can see the trade-off before opening a provider link.

## Who should use one

A VPS fits a small website, automation service, personal VPN, or a few containers when shared hosting feels too limiting. Beginners should start with a plan that leaves headroom for updates and backups rather than choosing the lowest number on the page.

| Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| RAM | More memory gives applications room for updates and concurrent work. |
| Effective monthly price | The normalized price makes different billing terms easier to compare. |
| Renewal price | A low first term can hide a higher ongoing cost. |
