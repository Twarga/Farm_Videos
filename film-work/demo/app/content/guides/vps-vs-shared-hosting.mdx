## The short answer

Shared hosting puts many websites behind one managed control panel. A VPS gives you a virtual machine with more control over the operating system and services. Neither is automatically better: the right choice depends on whether you want to trade maintenance time for control.

## Choose shared hosting when

Shared hosting is a sensible starting point for a small site when you want the host to handle most server administration. It can be a good fit for a brochure site, a low-traffic blog, or a simple site where a familiar control panel matters more than custom services.

The trade-off is less control over the environment. You usually work within the host's supported software and resource limits, so unusual background jobs or custom networking may not fit.

## Choose a VPS when

A VPS makes more sense when you need a custom service, containers, a private VPN, or control over the operating system. You also gain responsibility: updates, firewall rules, backups, monitoring, and recovery become your job unless you pay for management.

Start with the smallest plan that leaves room for your application and routine maintenance. A low monthly price is not a win if the machine is too small to update safely.

## A useful comparison

| Question | Shared hosting | VPS |
|---|---|---|
| Server maintenance | Mostly managed by the host | Mostly your responsibility |
| Software freedom | Limited to supported features | Broad, within provider rules |
| Starting complexity | Lower | Higher |
| First question to ask | Can the site run on the managed stack? | Can I operate this server safely? |
