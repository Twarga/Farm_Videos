## Compare the cost you will keep paying

A promotional first term can be useful, but it is not the same as the ongoing cost. Convert every billing period to an effective monthly price, then check the renewal price and the cash due today. A cheap headline can hide a long prepaid term or an expensive renewal.

## Use the same questions for every plan

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What is the effective monthly price? | Billing terms become comparable |
| What is due today? | Cash flow may differ from the monthly equivalent |
| What is the renewal price? | The ongoing cost may be higher |
| What extras are required? | IPv4, backups, panels, and setup can change the total |
| What facts are unknown? | Missing evidence should lower confidence, not become an estimate |

Do not compare a plan with included backups to one where backups are a separate purchase without showing that difference. Keep currencies and tax treatment visible as well.

## Read the catch beside the number

A price is only useful alongside RAM, CPU, storage, transfer, location, availability, and the source date. The [published catalog](/catalog?sort=effective) keeps those facts together and labels affiliate destinations. Its figures describe a dated snapshot, not a checkout guarantee.

## A repeatable habit

Write down the first-term total, renewal total, required extras, and cancellation terms. Recheck the provider source when you are ready to buy. If the source is unclear, mark the fact unknown instead of filling the gap with a guess.
