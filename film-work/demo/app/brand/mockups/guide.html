How to compare VPS prices without falling for the first-term number.

A four-step method for intro prices, contract terms, renewals and add-ons. It's the same method our catalog uses.

b/The bestvpsforme desk
Independent researchChecked 1 Sep 2026

On this page

  1. Find the term
  2. Find the renewal
  3. Add the extras
  4. Divide by 36

A VPS ad shows the smallest number the provider can legally print. That number is true. It just isn't what you'll pay. Here is how to find the number that is.

1. Find the term

Look for "with a 12-month term", "billed annually" or "24 months". If the intro price needs a long term, you pay for the whole term on day one.

Real example. Hostinger KVM 1 is advertised at $6.49/mo. It needs 24 months upfront: $155.76 due today.

2. Find the renewal

The renewal price is usually in the footnote or the checkout summary. It's the price you'll pay for most of the server's life.

3. Add the extras

IPv4 addresses, backups, snapshots and control panels are often extra. 40 plans in our snapshot charge extra for a public IPv4.

4. Divide by 36

Three years is roughly how long a small server lives. Add up the whole bill and divide by 36 months.

real_price = ( due_today + renewals + extras ) ÷ 36
$8.32 = ( $155.76 + 12 × $11.99 + $0 ) ÷ 36This is the "3-year real price" you see on every plan in the catalog.
