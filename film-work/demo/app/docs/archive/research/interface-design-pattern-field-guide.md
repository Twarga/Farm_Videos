# The Interface Taste Field Guide

## What this guide is for

This is not a gallery of websites to copy. It is a study of why nineteen very different products feel intentional, memorable, and alive. The aim is to make the hidden design decisions visible: positioning, information architecture, density, typography, shape language, product proof, motion, sponsorship, and the relationship between the interface and the product's personality.

The second purpose is practical. The guide turns the findings into a design system and page plan for **bestvpsforme.com**, then ends with a reusable method for designing future products without falling into generic AI-made layouts.

> **The central finding**
>
> Great product design is not a collection of attractive components. It is a coherent world. The promise, data, typography, imagery, layout, interactions, and business model all tell the same story.

## How the study was performed

Each site was inspected visually in a live browser and reviewed structurally through its public content and interactive surfaces. The analysis focuses on observable design decisions, not private conversion data or unverified implementation details. Websites change, so exact colors, content, prices, and layouts should be rechecked before implementation.

The sites were studied through six lenses:

1. **Premise:** the sharp idea the product owns.
2. **Visual grammar:** type, color, spacing, surfaces, imagery, and shape.
3. **Page architecture:** how the page moves from promise to proof to action.
4. **Product proof:** what real data or interface is shown.
5. **Interaction and motion:** what feels alive and why.
6. **Transferable lesson:** what can be reused without copying the site's appearance.

## The four design families

The references are different, but most combine four recurring families.

| Design family | Primary job | Strong examples | What makes it work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product theater | Demonstrate a product before signup | Warmbly, SuperX, Revise, MiroMiro, Dubbl, Zerops | The real interface becomes the visual asset. The page shows a workflow, not a decorative dashboard. |
| Living catalogue | Make a large database feel useful and alive | Nomads, Hotelist, Can I Vibecode It, Can I Replace It, BoilerplateHub | Search, filters, verdicts, activity, and inventory are part of the homepage identity. |
| Character world | Make a small product emotionally memorable | Goalmaxxing, Shinsei, Chorey, Levity | One metaphor controls the voice, illustration, type, color, and component behavior. |
| Conversion narrative | Move one audience toward one clear action | Liftoff, Indiedex, Effortless Backlinks, PocketUI | A direct promise, fast proof, low ambiguity, and repeated action with progressively stronger evidence. |

The best sites do not pick one family mechanically. They combine them around the product. BestVPSForMe should be a **living catalogue first**, with **product theater** in the finder and comparison flows, and a modest **character world** built around infrastructure confidence.

# Part I: The nineteen-site field study

## 1. Warmbly

**Observed visual signature:** a bright sky-blue hero, white navigation, enormous clean sans-serif typography, soft glass-like controls, and realistic product screens. The hero claim is unusually large and calm: the visual confidence comes from scale and restraint, not from many components.

### Why it feels impressive

Warmbly behaves like an application demonstration disguised as a landing page. After the open-source promise, the page repeatedly shows real product areas: campaign building, scheduling, inbox activity, analytics, AI help, integrations, and mobile behavior. The screenshots contain credible states, controls, data, and operational detail. This produces trust because the product appears to exist before the visitor is asked to believe marketing claims.

The sky treatment is also strategic. Cold email software normally looks dark, aggressive, or sales-heavy. Warmbly chooses air, daylight, and openness. That visual world supports its open-source and self-hosted positioning.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A hero environment, not a hero decoration.** The sky fills the stage and establishes the emotional temperature.
- **One large claim.** The headline is allowed to dominate; supporting text stays short.
- **Real product states.** Screens show mailboxes, latency, tables, scheduling, statuses, and controls.
- **Feature chapters.** Each major workflow gets a headline, a few concrete benefits, and its own interface proof.
- **High detail inside a simple frame.** The marketing shell is calm so the product UI can be dense.

### What not to copy

Do not copy the sky background simply because it is beautiful. It works because “open,” “cloud,” and “deliverability” already belong to the product story. A random sky behind VPS cards would be mood without meaning.

### BestVPS transfer

Show a real interactive **Deal Radar** in the homepage hero: a few current plans, real provider logos, location, effective price, workload verdict, and one visible caveat. Let the product prove its usefulness before explaining every feature.

Source: https://warmbly.com/

## 2. Nomads

**Observed visual signature:** a cinematic travel video as the hero, very large human promise, member avatars, bright coral action color, floating city activity tiles, and a dense data product immediately below.

### Why it feels impressive

Nomads sells belonging before it sells data. “Find your people” is supported by faces, meetups, chat, travel records, dating, and community activity. The hero is not a generic travel photograph. It behaves like a living scene with a signup card placed inside the world.

The catalogue below uses unusually high density: destination cards, city activity, deep filters, maps, comparison, search, AI help, and multiple data views. The density works because it represents the richness of the community and database. It feels active rather than cluttered.

### Patterns worth learning

- **Outcome before mechanism.** The page begins with human connection, not filters or city scores.
- **Evidence of life.** Avatars, meetup counts, city movement, and activity prevent the product from feeling like a static directory.
- **Several valid views of the same data.** Cards, map, comparison, scatter-style exploration, and search support different mental models.
- **Natural-language filters.** Safety, weather, cost, internet, and lifestyle speak to real decisions rather than database field names.
- **Density as identity.** Richness is not hidden in an “advanced” page.

### What not to copy

Nomads can tolerate visual noise because discovery and community activity are the product. A VPS visitor should not face hundreds of competing badges on first load. Borrow the sense of life and the multiple views, not the total density.

### BestVPS transfer

Add observable market activity without pretending to have a huge community: “12 prices rechecked this week,” “3 plans changed,” “popular for n8n,” “new Frankfurt stock,” or “most compared today.” These are useful, auditable signals of life.

Source: https://nomads.com/

## 3. Hotelist

**Observed visual signature:** black-and-white terminal-like typography, thick outlined filter controls, a severe split between list and map, small emoji-like metric labels, and almost no decorative marketing layer.

### Why it feels impressive

Hotelist owns a provocative thesis: no pay-to-play, only hotels rated by AI. That statement turns the interface into a tool with an editorial position. The design then reinforces the thesis with a stripped, almost forensic visual language. Filters, map, scores, normalized ratings, consensus, photos, pros and cons, and methodology carry more weight than brand decoration.

The strongest idea is not its monochrome style. It is the decision to make **trust mechanics visible**. The site explains how scores are normalized and exposes the difference between source ratings and its own judgment.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A visible editorial constitution.** The user immediately knows what the product refuses to do.
- **Tool-first homepage.** The catalogue is the homepage, not a secondary route.
- **Map and list as equal modes.** Geography is not a minor filter.
- **Methodology as interface.** Score explanations and consensus are part of the product experience.
- **Permanent, shareable state.** Search and filters can become a durable link.

### What not to copy

Monochrome terminal styling can become theatrical and hard to scan if the data hierarchy is weak. BestVPSForMe needs provider identity, country recognition, price emphasis, and accessibility. Use severity selectively.

### BestVPS transfer

Create an explicit policy line near results: **Affiliate status never changes organic ranking. Unknown data is shown as unknown. Prices display billing conditions.** This should be a functional trust strip linked to the ranking method, not a vague footer promise.

Source: https://hotelist.com/

## 4. SuperX

**Observed visual signature:** a dark charcoal canvas, thin architectural guide lines, bright white type, a controlled orange-red heat accent, large breathing room, and carefully staged product screenshots. The hero appears almost like a designed technical poster.

### Why it feels impressive

SuperX uses a familiar SaaS page sequence but executes it with discipline. The visual frame is consistent, screenshots look like real software, proof is numerical, and feature sections are grouped by the user journey: discover, create and schedule, engage and grow.

Its page rhythm matters. It alternates large statements, product frames, micro-benefits, and quantified testimonials. The repeated structure does not feel like a template because each chapter has its own spatial composition and evidence.

### Patterns worth learning

- **One accent with emotional meaning.** Orange behaves like heat, speed, and growth.
- **Architectural frame lines.** Fine rules and edge markers make the page feel engineered.
- **Journey-level chapters.** Features are grouped into meaningful stages instead of an undifferentiated grid.
- **Proof between chapters.** Testimonials and numbers interrupt marketing before belief gets tired.
- **Screenshots as hero art.** Product surfaces are lit, cropped, and composed with the same care as illustrations.

### What not to copy

Do not reproduce dark charcoal, orange gradients, and thin grid lines as a visual skin. Without a strong product structure, that becomes a generic “premium SaaS” costume.

### BestVPS transfer

Use restrained infrastructure lines to connect **requirements → eligible plans → comparison → decision**. The line system should explain a path through the tool, not merely decorate the background.

Source: https://superx.so/

## 5. Can I Vibecode It?

**Observed visual signature:** near-black background, Space Grotesk for the huge question, JetBrains Mono for the system voice, neon green status accents, colored sponsor cards fixed on both sides, a terminal search bar, category chips, a radio control, and deliberately visible inventory and scarcity.

### Why it feels impressive

This site has one of the strongest premises in the study. “Can I vibecode ___?” is both the product name, the search interaction, the headline, and the unit of content. One question organizes more than a thousand app verdicts.

The page also turns monetization into product design. Sponsors occupy ten fixed shelf positions around the central utility. They are visually distinct, always labeled, and sold through a transparent public inventory. The sponsor page publishes traffic, current availability, fixed pricing, suitable advertiser types, the purchase process, and the promise that sponsorship never changes a verdict.

The radio, terminal voice, “death list,” “escape hatches,” and exact prompts create a specific subculture. These elements are not random easter eggs. They all belong to builders deciding which subscriptions survive the AI era.

### Patterns worth learning deeply

- **One repeatable question.** Every search, page, headline, and verdict begins from the same grammar.
- **The interface speaks in a voice.** Small labels, status text, scarcity, and help copy all sound like the same person.
- **Sponsors are shelves, not interruptions.** Fixed side rails frame the utility without entering the verdict.
- **Public sponsor operations.** Availability and price are visible; buying a slot is a self-serve flow.
- **Radical transparency as conversion.** Live visits, audience type, testimonials, and placement rules reduce sales friction.
- **The database creates content architecture.** Apps, categories, alternatives, moats, builds, stats, and newsletters are related views of one knowledge graph.
- **A contribution loop.** Visitors can submit missing apps; the system drafts a verdict; the catalogue grows.
- **A retention loop.** Users can save the apps they are replacing and receive a weekly digest.
- **Visible numbers.** App count, alternatives count, traffic, slot count, and change cadence make the product feel real.

### The sponsor pattern, translated carefully

The side-rail model is especially relevant to BestVPSForMe, but copying it literally would be premature. A new VPS site should not show ten empty boxes and pretend demand exists. Start with one or two clearly labeled placements only when traffic is meaningful. Publish a sponsor policy before selling the first slot.

A future sponsor page should show:

1. Audience and geography in aggregate.
2. Placement preview at actual size.
3. Fixed duration and fixed price.
4. Current availability.
5. Allowed sponsor categories.
6. Human approval and refund rules.
7. The promise that sponsors cannot change rankings, verdicts, or data.
8. Click reporting without invasive tracking.

### BestVPS transfer

Own one repeatable question: **“What are you running?”** Every workload page, finder answer, result card, and comparison should connect back to it. Sponsor rails can appear only on very wide catalogue layouts, outside organic results, with a compact label such as “Partner shelf.”

Sources: https://canivibecodeit.com/ and https://canivibecodeit.com/sponsor

## 6. Liftoff

**Observed visual signature:** a plain white document-like page, compact navigation, heavy Nunito Sans, a blue selection-style highlight over “not months,” recognizable client logos, and short explanatory blocks.

### Why it feels impressive

Liftoff demonstrates that a page does not need elaborate art to look designed. It uses a single rhetorical device, the selected phrase, to make the time contrast memorable. The page then moves quickly into credibility and a practical explanation of scope.

Its restraint is useful because the offer is a service. Visitors want confidence, examples, timing, and a conversation, not an animated product universe.

### Patterns worth learning

- **One typographic gesture can carry the brand.** The selection highlight does more than a collection of gradients.
- **Recognizable proof is close to the promise.** Client logos appear before the user has to scroll far.
- **The page reads like a proposal.** It feels direct, personal, and low-friction.
- **Short navigation reflects a short decision path.** Our work and contact are enough.

### BestVPS transfer

Use a selection-style or inspection-style emphasis for the decisive part of a result: “best under €8,” “no contract,” or “lowest cash due today.” Do not highlight half the interface.

Source: https://liftoff.so/

## 7. Indiedex

**Observed visual signature:** crisp white background, deep blue and cyan typography, friendly Poppins, a two-column hero, live-looking member profile cards, Product Hunt proof, overlapping avatars, and a command-key prompt.

### Why it feels impressive

Indiedex does not explain community through abstract illustrations. It places actual community members beside the promise. The moving cursor in the headline, recently added cards, avatars, follower counts, and product statistics make “other indie hackers” concrete.

The command-key prompt is a small but strong detail: it signals that the product is searchable and that the catalogue has enough substance to query.

### Patterns worth learning

- **Show the people or objects named in the headline.** The right side contains members, not a generic dashboard.
- **Live inventory beside the promise.** “Recently added” makes the database current.
- **Friendly proof hierarchy.** Product Hunt, member count, avatars, and stars serve different trust needs.
- **One animated word can add life.** The cursor supports the meaning rather than decorating it.

### BestVPS transfer

Place real provider or plan cards beside a workload-driven hero, and rotate them through meaningful states: “best price,” “newly verified,” “price changed,” “strongest EU locations.”

Source: https://www.indiedex.app/

## 8. Levity

**Observed visual signature:** extreme white space, an elegant Instrument Serif headline, a small app identity mark, one black App Store button, and a large polished phone showing a sky-blue social fitness feed.

### Why it feels impressive

Levity lets one sentence and one product object occupy the whole first view. Nothing competes with them. The serif typography makes the promise feel human and reflective, while the phone provides enough evidence that the product is real.

This is the strongest example of **confidence through subtraction**. The composition is not empty because every remaining element has a clear job.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A single emotional promise.** “A day you can feel lift off” describes a state, not a feature.
- **One product object.** The phone becomes the entire illustration system.
- **Editorial type against app UI.** The contrast makes both feel more considered.
- **Large negative space.** Space is used as hierarchy, not wasted area.

### BestVPS transfer

Use subtraction on the beginner finder. One question per step, one recommendation concept at a time, and no catalogue noise while the user is describing their needs.

Source: https://levity.day/

## 9. Dubbl

**Observed visual signature:** a near-black emerald background, oversized editorial serif, bright mint italic emphasis, orbital line art, and small finance dashboards floating around the hero. The page feels like a modern financial control room.

### Why it feels impressive

Dubbl builds a visual world from its domain. Ledger entries, revenue charts, live feed events, asset allocation, invoice states, terminal commands, and balanced books are not stock decorations. They are the material of business management.

The type pairing is important: expressive serif language carries ambition, while compact interface panels carry precision. The result feels more premium than a normal dashboard landing page without losing technical credibility.

### Patterns worth learning

- **Domain artifacts as ornament.** Charts, journal entries, balances, and status events form the atmosphere.
- **Editorial plus operational typography.** Large serif statements sit beside small utilitarian data.
- **Live-feed motion.** Transaction events create a sense that the system is running.
- **Problem visualization.** Spreadsheet errors and mismatches are shown as a field of disorder before the unified solution.
- **Developer proof.** Git clone and API examples support the open-source promise.

### BestVPS transfer

Use domain artifacts around the interface: CPU allocation, RAM headroom, storage class, billing interval, route distance, and verification events. These should show meaningful states, not invented charts.

Source: https://dubbl.dev/

## 10. Can I Replace It?

**Observed visual signature:** dark navy utility, electric blue emphasis, a changing underlined product name, a compact statistics strip, sponsor ticker, sponsor wall, deep filters, dense result rows, and explicit independence language.

### Why it feels impressive

The premise is a judgment, not a directory category. Every paid product receives a verdict: replaceable, almost, or not yet. That verdict compresses a complex decision into a memorable system while detail pages can explain switching cost, openness, price, and alternatives.

The sponsor interface is also operationally mature. Slots are named, priced, limited, selectable, and bundled across surfaces. Sponsorship is labeled and explicitly separated from verdicts. The empty inventory is designed as part of the product rather than hidden behind a sales form.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A small verdict vocabulary.** Three states are easier to remember than dozens of scores.
- **Statistics establish authority.** Reviewed products, alternatives, negative verdicts, and reported switches show the system's scale.
- **Deep filters match a deep taxonomy.** Openness, effort, hosted availability, price, and category represent the real replacement decision.
- **Sponsor inventory is self-serve.** Placement, duration, quantity, and availability are visible.
- **Data policy is adjacent to results.** Licence, check dates, and editorial rules are not buried.

### What not to copy

BestVPSForMe should not force every plan into a single universal good/bad score. VPS fit depends on workload, location, budget, management ability, and risk tolerance.

### BestVPS transfer

Use a small contextual verdict set: **Strong fit, Good fit with a catch, Specialist fit, and Not eligible.** The verdict must always be tied to the current workload and filters.

Sources: https://canireplaceit.com/en/ and https://canireplaceit.com/en/sponsor

## 11. Revise

**Observed visual signature:** a clean white page, very large black headline, restrained blue action, and a complete document editor placed directly in the hero. File-type and model marks sit around the editor like supported objects.

### Why it feels impressive

Revise demonstrates the product immediately. The editor, formatting bar, empty page, AI agent, model selector, and upload state communicate more than a long feature list could. The user can mentally rehearse the workflow before signup.

The surrounding logos are not a generic “trusted by” strip. They represent compatible file types and AI ecosystems, so they extend the product explanation.

### Patterns worth learning

- **The first screen is almost the product.** Marketing and application share a visual language.
- **Empty state as demonstration.** “Paste or upload a file” teaches the first action.
- **Compatibility objects around the hero.** Integrations explain the edges of the product.
- **Low visual noise around a complex surface.** The editor gets the attention.

### BestVPS transfer

Let the homepage Deal Radar and finder use the same components as the full catalogue. Do not build a decorative fake comparison that behaves differently from the real product.

Source: https://revise.io/

## 12. Effortless Backlinks

**Observed visual signature:** bright white conversion page, deep navy text, vivid blue emphasis and CTA, founder avatars and stars, quantified promise, a floating testimonial video, benefit checks, and aggressive proof sequencing.

### Why it works

This is the most conversion-focused reference. Every element answers a buying objection: quality, manual work, number of links, domain rating, delivery time, proof, traffic relevance, process, pricing, testimonials, and refund-like reassurance.

It is less visually unusual than Goalmaxxing or Can I Vibecode It, but the page is effective because the offer is unusually specific. The design spends its energy on certainty rather than personality.

### Patterns worth learning

- **Quantified promise in the headline.** One hundred links, thirty days, manual submission.
- **Proof appears in several forms.** Counts, logos, screenshots, testimonials, video, report preview, and process.
- **The CTA repeats with stronger context.** The first action is ambition; later actions are supported by evidence.
- **The deliverable is visible.** The report screenshot proves what the buyer receives.
- **Price is anchored to labor saved.** The offer frames the avoided work.

### What not to copy

Do not use inflated founder avatars, five-star strips, or floating testimonial cards before BestVPSForMe has real users. False social proof destroys the trust advantage.

### BestVPS transfer

Use proof that exists: plans checked, providers covered, fields verified, price changes detected, and sample source records. Replace generic “trusted by” language with auditable operational numbers.

Source: https://www.effortlessbacklinks.com/

## 13. BoilerplateHub

**Observed visual signature:** black catalogue, magenta identity, a concise marketing hero, filters and search immediately below, followed by large visual listing cards with stack logos, features, maker proof, price, badges, and sponsor panels.

### Why it feels useful

BoilerplateHub understands that people need to compare objects with many attributes. The hero quickly hands control to the catalogue. The listings combine a product screenshot with structured technical data, social proof, maker identity, and a price range.

The page also integrates sponsors inside the broader catalogue rhythm while labeling them. Its main strength is the transition from promise to inventory without a long feature-story detour.

### Patterns worth learning

- **Marketing collapses into utility.** Search and filters are visible in the first viewport.
- **Logos replace text where recognition is faster.** Framework and feature icons reduce scanning cost.
- **Cards have a repeated reading order.** Identity, stack, capabilities, maker, trust, price, action.
- **Badges are scarce and meaningful.** “Most popular” and “verified partner” signal a specific status.
- **A product image remains important in a dense catalogue.** Visual recognition prevents every entry from becoming the same row.

### BestVPS transfer

Provider logos, country flags, CPU vendor marks, storage type icons, operating-system marks, and control-panel compatibility can make scanning faster. Use them only when they represent real data and include text labels or accessible names.

Source: https://boilerplatehub.com/

## 14. Zerops

**Observed visual signature:** pale technical canvas, centered green-black typography, vast empty first screen, a single black onboarding bar containing coding-agent icons, and a detailed animated infrastructure story below.

### Why it feels impressive

Zerops is quiet at first and extremely technical later. The first view makes one category claim: a cloud platform for humans and their coding agents. The rest of the page then earns that claim through a narrated system sequence: databases, Redis, queues, development, staging, pull requests, approval, production, networking, load balancers, metrics, and billing.

This is information design rather than screenshot marketing. The system architecture itself becomes the animation and illustration.

### Patterns worth learning

- **One strong sentence in a large field of space.** The category claim is given time to land.
- **A CTA that previews compatibility.** Agent logos inside the action answer “does it work with mine?”
- **Infrastructure as a story.** Provisioning and deployment are shown as a sequence.
- **Visual systems diagram.** Private networks, services, and environments are spatially explained.
- **A contrarian pricing explanation.** Bring-your-own subscription is made visible as a calculation.

### BestVPS transfer

Animate the finder as a transparent reasoning sequence: workload minimums appear, incompatible plans drop out, location narrows the set, contract preference removes offers, and the shortlist settles. The user should understand the filtering logic without reading an algorithm document.

Source: https://zerops.io/

## 15. MiroMiro

**Observed visual signature:** a rounded luminous blue theater framed inside a white page, vertical light bands, large centered white headline, trust pill, paired calls to action, and tilted product panels rising from the bottom edge.

### Why it feels impressive

MiroMiro presents a strong visual transformation: stop rebuilding from screenshots, use real code. The hero looks like a stage where the extraction product is being revealed. The blue environment creates depth, while the white browser-extension panels provide crisp product proof.

The page then repeats a disciplined feature system: short label, specific action, explanation, benefit bullets, and a product image or demo. Testimonials describe workflow value, not only enthusiasm.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A bounded theater.** The hero is a large visual object inside the page, not a full-page gradient.
- **Foreground product panels.** Cropped UI creates depth without fake 3D devices.
- **Trust pill before the claim.** Rating and user count prepare belief.
- **Two clear entry paths.** Browser extension and API reflect two real audiences.
- **Feature demonstrations preserve a consistent camera angle.** The page feels directed.

### BestVPS transfer

Use a bounded dark **comparison theater** on the light homepage. Inside it, animate the transition from many offers to a small shortlist. Keep the rest of the page calm so this single high-impact object earns attention.

Source: https://miromiro.app/

## 16. PocketUI

**Observed visual signature:** very clean white canvas, centered black headline, soft lavender action color, a gentle word-level treatment of “taste,” thin structural grid lines, and a four-column capability strip at the bottom of the first view.

### Why it feels impressive

PocketUI uses quiet structure. The page is intentionally softer and less cinematic than MiroMiro even though the products are adjacent. The typography, low-contrast borders, lavender, and centered form make it feel like a personal library rather than an extraction machine.

Its best copy and design decision is to treat taste as persistent data that an AI can use. The feature grid supports that story: save, export to code, export to Figma, and organize a library.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A soft category metaphor.** “Give your AI taste” is easier to remember than a technical feature list.
- **Low-contrast structural lines.** The grid organizes space without becoming decoration.
- **The first viewport includes the product model.** Four capabilities establish the system after the promise.
- **One gentle accent.** Lavender is used for action and atmosphere, not every component.

### BestVPS transfer

Create a small, memorable category metaphor such as **“Your server shortlist, explained.”** Keep beginner onboarding visually quiet; save high density for the workbench.

Source: https://pocketui.app/

## 17. Goalmaxxing

**Observed visual signature:** warm off-white graph paper, a black-and-white muscular crocodile mascot, huge heavy black typography, blunt conversational copy, large tactile App Store and Discord cards, and a visible scarcity counter.

### Why it feels impressive

Goalmaxxing has a complete character world. The mascot is not an icon in a rounded square. Max roasts goals, reduces tasks, awards XP, prints weekly receipts, and appears in the product logic. The graph paper, training language, receipt metaphor, progression, and forceful type all reinforce the same idea: goals become physical things you move.

The opening has almost no normal SaaS language. “Stuck? Let's fix it.” is direct, emotional, and actionable.

### Patterns worth learning

- **A mascot with a job.** Max performs product actions and carries the voice.
- **A physical metaphor.** Goals are pushed, roasted, simplified, swapped, and measured.
- **Unpolished warmth.** Graph paper and ink illustration create a handmade counterpoint to the app.
- **Tactile action blocks.** QR and Discord are large destination cards, not tiny footer links.
- **Scarcity belongs to the world.** Pro seats feel like membership in a small training crew.

### What not to copy

BestVPSForMe does not need a cartoon server mascot unless that character can genuinely teach, warn, and guide throughout the product. Personality should not reduce trust in price and infrastructure data.

### BestVPS transfer

Use a restrained guide voice that performs a job: it translates technical facts, calls out unsafe assumptions, and explains catches. The “character” can be the **Infrastructure Guide** rather than a literal mascot.

Source: https://goalmaxxxing.app/

## 18. Shinsei

**Observed visual signature:** cinematic near-black canvas, faint warm glow, large orange editorial serif headline, small uppercase labels, a tall dark phone, restrained social proof, and minimal navigation.

### Why it feels impressive

Shinsei sells self-discovery, so the page feels private, nocturnal, and reflective. The headline “Break the Usual Script” is not a feature description. It creates tension, then the phone shows a quiet, text-led application that appears to observe patterns over time.

The product story is organized around discover, learn, and act. The emotional atmosphere remains consistent across the landing page and the app surface.

### Patterns worth learning

- **Mood matches the psychological job.** Darkness suggests privacy and introspection.
- **Editorial typography carries emotion.** The orange serif is the primary visual asset.
- **Small proof does not break the mood.** Member faces and count remain low-key.
- **Three-part behavioral loop.** Discover, learn, and act explain the system without a feature grid.

### BestVPS transfer

Use emotional tone deliberately for high-risk moments. Backup warnings, data-loss risks, and unmanaged-server responsibility should feel sober and calm, not colorful or playful.

Source: https://shinsei.app/

## 19. Chorey

**Observed visual signature:** warm cream background, Bricolage Grotesque headline, Plus Jakarta Sans body, heavy friendly outlines, coral, lavender, and green accents, tag-like chips, and a simplified phone illustration that demonstrates the exact parent action.

### Why it feels impressive

Chorey turns the whole product into one memorable mechanism: approve a chore, then split the reward into spend, save, and give. The hero illustration shows the button, the three buckets, the amounts, and the claim that the button is the whole parent job.

The design feels friendly without becoming childish. Thick outlines and soft color support family use, while the copy is precise about approval, codes, no child email, no bank account, and no real money movement.

### Patterns worth learning

- **One mechanism explains the product.** Approval and 40/40/20 appear everywhere.
- **Illustration is functional.** The phone mockup teaches the action.
- **Color has stable meaning.** Spend, save, and give keep their identities.
- **Friendly shapes support serious rules.** Privacy and money constraints remain clear.
- **The parent and child experiences are separated.** Each audience gets its own explanation.

### BestVPS transfer

Give persistent colors to a very small set of meanings: good fit, catch, unknown, sponsored, and changed. Never allow provider brand colors to replace semantic status colors.

Source: https://chorey.co/

# Part II: Why these interfaces feel non-generic

## 20. They begin with a sharp product sentence

Distinctive design usually begins before Figma. The strongest references can be compressed into a sentence that already implies an interface:

- Can I Vibecode It asks one repeatable question.
- Hotelist promises no pay-to-play judgment.
- Chorey makes one approval split money three ways.
- Goalmaxxing focuses on one goal and one next move.
- Levity promises a day that feels lighter.
- Zerops makes coding agents operate real infrastructure.

When the sentence is vague, the design agent fills the gap with familiar components. A sharper premise reduces visual randomness.

## 21. They have a governing metaphor

A governing metaphor is an idea that controls several layers at once.

| Product | Governing metaphor | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can I Vibecode It | Terminal verdict board | Search, monospaced voice, neon status, sponsor shelves, death list, radio |
| Goalmaxxing | Physical training for a goal | Mascot, graph paper, roast, XP, receipts, scarcity, blunt language |
| Dubbl | Living financial control room | Ledger events, charts, orbital lines, emerald palette, terminal commands |
| Chorey | One tap into three jars | Illustration, colors, copy, hierarchy, onboarding, progress |
| MiroMiro | Website extraction theater | Luminous stage, inspector panels, code transition, demos |
| BestVPSForMe proposal | Infrastructure workbench | Requirements, eligibility, plan ledger, source checks, comparison tray, guide voice |

The metaphor should not be a costume. It must help explain the product.

## 22. They use product-specific artifacts

Generic AI design uses abstract gradients, stock illustrations, and icon cards because it lacks domain material. Strong interfaces use artifacts that only their product could contain:

- Warmbly: campaign schedules, mailboxes, warmup status, inbox triage.
- Nomads: cities, meetups, weather, community faces, maps.
- Dubbl: journal entries, reconciliation, invoices, balances.
- Revise: document canvas, tracked changes, model selector, file types.
- Zerops: databases, queues, environments, pull requests, networks.
- Chorey: chores, approval, spend/save/give jars.

For BestVPSForMe, the visual material already exists: provider marks, prices, billing terms, cores, RAM, storage, transfer, regions, virtualization, backup, IP fees, source checks, price changes, and workload fit. There is no need to invent decorative clouds.

## 23. They control density instead of avoiding it

There are three useful density modes:

1. **Focus mode:** one decision, large type, large space. Levity and Zerops use it in the hero.
2. **Narrative mode:** medium density, alternating proof and explanation. SuperX and MiroMiro use it for feature stories.
3. **Workbench mode:** high density, persistent controls, repeated rows, multiple views. Nomads, Hotelist, and the catalogue products use it for exploration.

A complete product should deliberately move between modes. BestVPSForMe should use focus mode in the finder, narrative mode on the homepage and plan explanation, and workbench mode in the catalogue and comparison.

## 24. Their typography has a job

Typography becomes memorable when the type role matches the product role.

- Editorial serifs create emotion or authority: Levity, Dubbl, Shinsei.
- Geometric sans-serif creates direct product confidence: Warmbly, SuperX, Indiedex.
- Monospace creates system voice and operational texture: Can I Vibecode It, Hotelist.
- Friendly grotesques create warmth without losing structure: Chorey, Goalmaxxing.

The lesson is not “use two fonts.” It is to define a **display voice**, a **reading voice**, and a **data voice**. BestVPSForMe needs:

- A confident grotesque display face for questions and verdicts.
- A highly readable sans-serif for explanations and interface labels.
- A tabular-number or mono-capable data face for prices, specs, IDs, and source history.

## 25. Their shape language is narrow

Memorable systems repeat a small vocabulary:

- Goalmaxxing uses paper, ink, soft rectangles, and heavy type.
- Chorey uses thick outlines, friendly rounded rectangles, and colored tags.
- SuperX uses dark panels, fine rules, and restrained rounded actions.
- Hotelist uses hard black surfaces and thick outlined controls.
- PocketUI uses subtle lines, soft lavender actions, and very quiet cards.

Generic interfaces often mix pills, glass cards, sharp tables, playful blobs, and floating icons in one page. Pick two or three surface types and use them consistently.

## 26. Their motion has semantic meaning

Useful motion answers a question:

- What is changing? A rotating product name or price-change ticker.
- What is alive? A live feed, city activity, or verification pulse.
- What happened because of me? A result being filtered out or added to compare.
- Where did this come from? A plan row expanding into a detail drawer.
- What should I notice? A single changing word or highlighted metric.

Avoid ambient motion on every card. A comparison tool must preserve reading stability.

## 27. Their proof is embedded, not postponed

The strongest pages place proof next to the exact claim it supports.

| Claim type | Useful proof |
| --- | --- |
| Product works | Real application state, interactive sample, short demo |
| Data is trustworthy | Source link, checked date, methodology, change history |
| People use it | Real count, recognizable activity, named testimonial |
| Service delivers | Sample report, process, before/after evidence |
| Sponsor placement works | Public traffic, slot preview, qualified-click example |
| Price is honest | Billing interval, cash due, renewal, tax, fees |

For BestVPSForMe, a freshness date beside each recommendation is stronger proof than a generic security badge.

# Part III: Design direction for bestvpsforme.com

## 28. Product design thesis

**BestVPSForMe should feel like an infrastructure workbench explained by a patient technical buyer.** It should combine the calm confidence of a premium editorial product with the speed and density of an operations console.

It should not look like:

- A hosting-company pricing page.
- An affiliate blog with blue buttons and star ratings.
- A spreadsheet placed inside generic rounded cards.
- A neon hacker dashboard.
- A purple-gradient SaaS template.
- A clone of servers.fyi, Can I Vibecode It, or any one reference.

## 29. The governing metaphor: the infrastructure workbench

The workbench has five parts:

1. **Requirement board:** what the user wants to run and what it safely requires.
2. **Deal radar:** current plans entering and leaving eligibility as criteria change.
3. **Plan ledger:** the facts, costs, unknowns, sources, and last verification.
4. **Comparison bench:** two to four plans aligned by decision-relevant differences.
5. **Guide voice:** plain-English interpretation of fit, catch, and risk.

This metaphor supports both beginners and technical buyers. Beginners can follow the guide; experts can use the bench directly.

## 30. Proposed visual world

### Color

Use a warm, technical neutral foundation rather than pure SaaS white.

| Role | Proposed direction | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | Warm mineral white | Homepage, explanation, detail reading |
| Ink | Deep green-black | Primary text, dark workbench surfaces |
| Signal | Electric coral or vermilion | Primary action, changed values, active reasoning path |
| Confidence | Clear green | Verified and strong fit only |
| Caution | Amber | Important catch or limited evidence |
| Unknown | Cool grey-violet | Missing data and uncertainty |
| Sponsor | Muted blue | Partner labels and sponsor inventory, never organic ranking |

Use provider brand colors only in provider logos or narrow identity marks. Semantic colors must remain stable.

### Typography

- **Display:** a wide, confident grotesque with strong numerals and several weights.
- **UI/body:** a neutral sans-serif optimized for 14-18 px reading.
- **Data:** tabular numerals; use monospace selectively for plan IDs, prices, source records, and technical values.
- **Rule:** a price and a paragraph must never compete at the same typographic level.

### Shapes

- Main workbench: low-radius or medium-radius structural panels.
- Interactive controls: compact rounded rectangles, not pill soup.
- Status: small flags or tags with icons and text.
- Comparison: strong column rules and sticky anchors.
- Detail reading: open paper surfaces with fewer containers.

### Texture and imagery

- Provider logos and data are primary visual assets.
- Country flags may aid recognition but must not replace country names.
- Infrastructure diagrams should be simplified and explanatory.
- A subtle rack-grid or routing-line motif may appear in major transitions.
- Avoid stock data-center photographs unless used editorially in provider history.

## 31. Homepage architecture

### First viewport

The homepage should be useful immediately.

**Header:** logo, Browse plans, Finder, Compare, Guides, methodology, search, theme. Keep it compact.

**Question:** “What are you running?” with a large command-style input and common workload chips: n8n, Coolify, WordPress, Docker apps, game server, VPN, development box, “I only know my specs.”

**Promise:** “A short VPS list with the real cost, the catch, and the reason.”

**Action pair:** Find my VPS and Browse all plans.

**Deal Radar:** a real compact table of five to eight plans, with provider logo, plan, location, safe-fit label, RAM, CPU class, storage, effective monthly price, cash due, checked date, and compare control.

The Deal Radar must use the production catalogue component, not a fake marketing imitation.

### Homepage sequence

1. Useful first viewport and Deal Radar.
2. “From workload to shortlist” reasoning animation.
3. Three recommendation examples: cheapest safe, balanced, easier to manage.
4. Price truth: advertised price versus effective cost and cash due.
5. Location and latency view.
6. Trust constitution and visible source example.
7. Workload collections.
8. Recent market changes.
9. Beginner guides.
10. Newsletter or price-change alert.

Do not add a generic three-card feature grid. Every section must show a decision or artifact.

## 32. Catalogue architecture

### Desktop layout

- Left: persistent filter rail, approximately 260-300 px.
- Center: main results in table or card mode.
- Top: search, result count, active filters, sort, view switch, share, reset.
- Bottom or side: persistent comparison tray after the first selection.
- Outside rail at very large widths: optional partner shelf, never inside organic result order.

### Filter groups

Filters should match actual VPS decisions:

**Workload and safety**

- Workload preset.
- Minimum recommended RAM and headroom.
- Minimum usable CPU class.
- Minimum storage and storage type.
- Managed or unmanaged comfort.

**Price truth**

- Effective monthly price.
- Cash due today.
- Billing interval.
- Minimum commitment.
- Setup fee.
- IPv4 included or charged.
- Renewal price known.
- Tax included, excluded, or unknown.
- Promo versus standard price.

**Compute**

- vCPU count.
- Shared, dedicated, or unknown CPU.
- CPU vendor or architecture.
- Virtualization type.
- Burst policy known.

**Memory and storage**

- RAM.
- ECC known.
- Disk size.
- NVMe, SSD, HDD, or network block storage.
- Included snapshots or backups.

**Network**

- Transfer allowance.
- Port speed.
- Inbound and outbound accounting.
- IPv4 and IPv6.
- DDoS protection known.

**Location**

- Continent, country, city, and region.
- Exact location versus provider-wide availability.
- Data residency requirement.

**Provider and operations**

- Provider.
- Founded or maturity information where useful.
- Support channel.
- SLA known.
- Control-panel compatibility.
- API or Terraform support.
- Refund window.

**Evidence**

- Checked within 7, 30, or 90 days.
- Complete cost data.
- Complete location data.
- Unknown-field threshold.
- Affiliate link available, visible as information only, never a ranking filter by default.

### Table mode

The table is for speed. It needs:

- Sticky header and first identity column.
- Provider logo plus plan name.
- One-line contextual verdict.
- Effective monthly cost and cash due today.
- Specs aligned with tabular numbers.
- Location summary with expansion.
- Catch or unknown marker.
- Checked date.
- Compare checkbox.
- Row expansion for secondary fields.

Do not show thirty columns at once. Offer column sets: **Essential, Performance, Network, Billing, Evidence**, plus a custom set.

### Card mode

Cards are for recognition and explanation, not for hiding fewer fields in a larger box.

Each card should contain:

1. Provider logo, plan name, and region.
2. Contextual fit verdict.
3. Four core specs in a stable grid.
4. Effective price, cash due, and billing interval.
5. One strongest reason.
6. One main catch.
7. Confidence and checked date.
8. Compare and open detail actions.

Cards should not have equal visual weight for every field. Price, fit, and catch lead; raw metadata follows.

## 33. Finder architecture

The beginner finder uses focus mode. Ask one main question per step and show why it matters.

1. **What are you running?** Choose workload or describe it.
2. **How much can you spend?** Monthly comfort plus maximum cash due today.
3. **Where are your users or data?** Country, region, or “no preference.”
4. **How much server management are you comfortable with?** Beginner, willing to learn, comfortable, expert.
5. **Important preferences.** Contract, backup, storage, traffic, Windows, ARM, IPv4.
6. **Transparent reasoning.** Show requirements and exclusions settling into a shortlist.

### Result design

Return three roles instead of a numbered popularity list:

- **Cheapest safe fit.** Lowest valid cost after eligibility.
- **Balanced choice.** Strong headroom, evidence, and price.
- **Easier choice.** Better operational experience or management options.

Each result must show:

- Why it fits.
- Main catch.
- Real cost and payment timing.
- Location.
- Evidence freshness.
- Important assumptions.
- Compare action.
- Direct or affiliate-link disclosure.

If nothing is safe, say so and recommend changing the requirements or choosing a managed alternative.

## 34. Plan detail page

Every VPS plan needs a permanent, indexable detail page.

### Page sequence

1. Identity: provider logo, plan name, location availability, price, checked date.
2. Contextual verdict: strongest fit, catch, confidence.
3. Primary actions: compare, visit provider, save or share.
4. Specification ledger.
5. Cost breakdown and billing timeline.
6. Location and network availability.
7. Workload fit matrix.
8. Operational difficulty and support.
9. Backups, snapshots, recovery, and data-loss cautions.
10. Source record and change history.
11. Similar plans by role, not simply price.
12. Affiliate disclosure beside the outbound action.

### Cost visualization

Show a twelve-month payment line:

- Cash due now.
- Recurring payment points.
- Setup and IP fees.
- Promo end.
- Renewal change.
- Estimated first-year total.

If a value is unknown, mark it unknown. Do not draw a false smooth line.

## 35. Comparison page

The comparison page is a decision surface, not a marketing page.

- Two to four plan columns.
- Sticky provider identities and prices.
- Row groups: fit, real cost, compute, memory, storage, network, locations, operations, evidence.
- Difference-only toggle.
- “Best in row” highlights only when the comparison is valid.
- Unknowns never lose silently to known values.
- Plain-English conclusion at the top and bottom.
- Shareable URL.
- Mobile uses one anchored reference plan and horizontal or stepwise comparison, not a crushed four-column table.

## 36. Provider page

A provider page should answer:

- What kind of buyer the provider suits.
- Regions and location precision.
- General billing patterns.
- Support and operational model.
- Relevant policies and caveats.
- Affiliate or partnership status.
- All active plans.
- Recent plan and price changes.
- Provider-level evidence and last review.

Provider pages must not read like sponsored biographies. Use the same editorial constitution as plan pages.

## 37. Admin as an evidence workbench

The founder's admin should not be a generic CRUD dashboard. It should feel like an operations desk organized around risk and freshness.

### Home view

- Plans that need verification.
- Detected price or spec changes.
- Broken provider and affiliate links.
- Unknown fields blocking recommendations.
- Most-clicked plans and workload paths.
- Provider coverage gaps.
- Sponsor inventory and upcoming expiry.
- Recent manual actions.

### Plan editor

- Current public record beside source evidence.
- Changed fields highlighted.
- Effective date and checked date separated.
- Cost calculator preview.
- Workload eligibility preview.
- Public card and detail preview.
- Affiliate link and direct link side by side.
- Draft, verified, stale, and archived states.
- Audit history and rollback.

### AI assistance

AI may extract provider-page candidates, summarize terms, map fields, flag contradictions, and draft plain-English catches. It must not publish prices, rankings, sponsor placements, or safety claims without human approval.

## 38. Sponsor and affiliate design

### Affiliate links

- Label the relationship beside the outbound action.
- Provide a direct-link option when practical.
- Never use affiliate availability in eligibility or organic sorting.
- Record click events by plan, provider, workload, position, and disclosure state.
- Do not claim conversion without provider evidence.

### Sponsorship

Start only when there is real traffic and an audience story.

Recommended future inventory:

- One homepage partner showcase below the Deal Radar.
- One category or workload partner slot.
- One optional left or right partner shelf on very wide catalogue screens.
- One newsletter placement.

Avoid sponsored cards inserted as if they were organic VPS results.

Every placement must show **Sponsored** or **Partner** at the first reading level, not only in a tooltip.

### Sponsor page

Publish placement previews, duration, fixed price, availability, approval rules, audience aggregates, and editorial separation. Do not copy another site's price. Price should be based on BestVPSForMe's own qualified traffic and click quality.

## 39. Motion plan

| Motion | Purpose | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finder elimination | Explain reasoning | Plans fade and slide out as a visible criterion fails; reduced-motion mode updates instantly |
| Comparison tray | Preserve spatial continuity | Selected plan mark travels into a persistent tray |
| Price change | Draw attention to new evidence | Old price briefly remains as a ghost before new price settles |
| Verification pulse | Show freshness event | One restrained pulse on a newly checked badge, never continuous |
| Row expansion | Reveal detail without losing place | Height expands from the row with stable columns |
| View switch | Preserve data identity | Items morph between table and card positions when practical |
| Sponsor rail | Maintain stability | No auto-rotation; fixed placement prevents distraction and improves reporting |

Motion should be short, interruptible, and absent where users need to compare numbers without movement.

## 40. Responsive behavior

### Mobile

- Finder becomes the primary entry point.
- Catalogue filters move into a bottom sheet with result-count preview.
- Active filters remain as a horizontally scrollable summary.
- Table mode becomes a compact ledger with user-selected columns.
- Comparison uses an anchored reference plan and one challenger at a time.
- Partner shelves move below organic content and remain labeled.
- Important cost facts must remain visible without opening a card.

### Tablet

- Filter rail can collapse into a persistent drawer.
- Card mode can use two columns.
- Comparison supports two full columns.

### Wide desktop

- Persistent filter rail and compare tray.
- Optional partner shelf outside organic results.
- Dense table column sets and keyboard shortcuts.

## 41. Accessibility rules

- Status never depends on color alone.
- Provider logos always have text alternatives.
- Flags never replace country names.
- Data cells preserve a logical reading order.
- Filter controls expose active values and result-count changes.
- Keyboard navigation covers search, filters, sort, view, compare, row expansion, and detail actions.
- Motion respects reduced-motion preferences.
- Minimum touch targets remain comfortable on mobile.
- “Unknown,” “not applicable,” and “not verified” are distinct states.
- Sponsor labels are read by assistive technology before sponsor content.

# Part IV: A reusable design method for future products

## 42. The anti-generic design sequence

Use this sequence before asking any design AI to create screens.

### Step 1: Write the sharp premise

Complete: **For [specific person], this product turns [painful situation] into [clear outcome] by [distinct mechanism].**

If the mechanism is vague, stop. More visual references will not fix it.

### Step 2: Choose the governing metaphor

Ask:

- What physical place does the product resemble: workbench, studio, cockpit, library, field guide, market, coach, ledger?
- What objects naturally exist there?
- Which interface behaviors does the metaphor improve?
- Which metaphors would mislead the user?

### Step 3: Inventory domain artifacts

List twenty real objects, data points, actions, states, and pieces of evidence from the domain. Use these before creating decorative art.

### Step 4: Define density modes

Mark each route or section as focus, narrative, or workbench. Do not use the same card density everywhere.

### Step 5: Define the voice roles

- Display voice: emotional promise and verdicts.
- Reading voice: explanations and guidance.
- Data voice: numbers, states, IDs, and evidence.

### Step 6: Define one shape language

Choose:

- Base surface.
- Work surface.
- Control shape.
- Status shape.
- Image or product frame.
- Divider language.

Ban extra shapes unless a real interaction needs them.

### Step 7: Place product proof

For every major claim, specify the proof beside it. If no proof exists, reduce the claim.

### Step 8: Storyboard motion

Motion must explain state, causality, continuity, or priority. Do not request “smooth animations” as a general requirement.

### Step 9: Design monetization honestly

Define sponsor and affiliate placement before traffic arrives so revenue cannot later invade organic results invisibly.

### Step 10: Run the genericity audit

Reject the first design if it relies on several of these:

- Left-aligned headline plus random dashboard on the right.
- Purple or blue gradient with no product meaning.
- Three equal benefit cards with icon containers.
- Pills used for every label and control.
- Endless rounded rectangles with identical hierarchy.
- Decorative analytics that do not correspond to product data.
- Fake user avatars or star ratings.
- Repeated centered sections with the same width and rhythm.
- Abstract blobs instead of domain artifacts.
- Screenshots too small to read.
- Generic “save time, grow faster, all-in-one” copy.
- Motion on every element but no explanation of state.

## 43. How to combine references without cloning

Use a **pattern recipe**, not a mood-board average.

Example for BestVPSForMe:

- From Can I Vibecode It: a repeatable question and transparent sponsor shelves.
- From Hotelist: visible methodology, source confidence, and list/map equality.
- From Nomads: multiple ways to explore data and observable market activity.
- From SuperX: architectural framing and journey-level page rhythm.
- From Warmbly and Revise: real product components as hero proof.
- From Zerops: transparent reasoning animation.
- From Chorey: stable semantic colors and a mechanism that explains itself.
- From Levity: quiet, focused beginner steps.

The result should not visually resemble any one reference. The combination is governed by the infrastructure workbench metaphor.

## 44. Design review scorecard

Score each major page out of 100.

| Dimension | Points | Approval question |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Product specificity | 20 | Could this design belong to an unrelated SaaS product after changing the logo? |
| Decision clarity | 20 | Can the target user identify the next useful action and understand the result? |
| Information hierarchy | 15 | Do price, fit, catch, and evidence beat decorative content? |
| Product proof | 15 | Are real data, states, or workflows visible beside the claims? |
| System coherence | 15 | Do type, color, shapes, copy, and motion belong to one world? |
| Accessibility and responsive behavior | 10 | Can the key job be completed by keyboard, screen reader, and mobile user? |
| Motion quality | 5 | Does motion explain something and respect stability? |

**Approval threshold:** 80 overall, with no score below half in any dimension. A beautiful page that cannot support the product decision fails.

## 45. The design-AI brief template

Give a design agent this structure:

1. Product premise and mechanism.
2. Primary user and exact decision.
3. Required data and states.
4. Governing metaphor.
5. Density mode for the route.
6. Real artifacts to show.
7. Reference patterns to borrow, with a reason for each.
8. Patterns explicitly banned.
9. Responsive behavior.
10. Accessibility requirements.
11. Motion storyboard.
12. Monetization boundaries.
13. Acceptance scorecard.

Ask for three clearly different directions at the **system level**, not three color variations. Each direction must change the governing metaphor, page composition, and information hierarchy.

## 46. Feature-design worksheet

Before designing any new feature, answer:

- What user decision or action becomes easier?
- What information is required?
- What can be unknown, stale, or contradictory?
- What is the empty state?
- What is the loading state?
- What is the error state?
- What is the most dangerous misunderstanding?
- What is the mobile version of the core job?
- What evidence proves the feature's output?
- What event should be measured?
- Does the feature need focus, narrative, or workbench density?
- Which existing components and semantic colors apply?
- What must never be influenced by revenue?

## 47. First design deliverables for BestVPSForMe

The next designer should produce these in order:

1. One-page product and design thesis.
2. Information architecture and route map.
3. Data hierarchy for plan, provider, workload, price, location, and evidence.
4. Three system-level visual directions.
5. Chosen type, color, shape, and icon grammar.
6. Desktop catalogue at realistic density with at least twelve plans.
7. Mobile catalogue and filter sheet.
8. Finder flow and transparent-reasoning transition.
9. Plan detail page with cost timeline and source history.
10. Four-plan comparison and mobile comparison behavior.
11. Homepage using real catalogue components.
12. Admin evidence workbench.
13. Sponsor and affiliate disclosure states.
14. Motion storyboard and reduced-motion version.
15. Component states, accessibility notes, and content rules.

Use real or plausible VPS data, long provider names, multiple currencies, unknown fields, expired promos, and price changes. A design tested only with perfect short content is not ready.

# Conclusion

The nineteen references do not share one visual style. Some are cinematic, some severe, some playful, some almost empty, and some extremely dense. Their common quality is stronger: each product makes a small number of decisions and repeats them with discipline.

BestVPSForMe can reach the same level without a large budget by investing in the right things:

- A sharp question: what are you running?
- A visible editorial constitution.
- Real provider identity and real data.
- A workbench that respects both beginners and experts.
- Clear price truth, catches, unknowns, and source freshness.
- A narrow type, color, and shape grammar.
- Motion that explains reasoning.
- Monetization designed outside organic judgment.
- A founder admin built around evidence, not database forms.

The goal is not to look expensive. The goal is to feel inevitable: when a visitor sees the product, the interface should seem like the natural way VPS buying always should have worked.
