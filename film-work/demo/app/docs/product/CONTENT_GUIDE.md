# Guides editorial guide

## Public name and purpose

Use **Guides** in navigation, page titles, and internal links. Use **Research** in a title when an article reports a measured dataset result. Do not present the section as a company-news blog.

Guides answer a specific question for someone choosing, configuring, or checking a VPS. Each article should help a reader take one useful next step in the catalog, methodology, or corrections path.

## Audience

Write for a beginner who can compare a monthly price but may not know the difference between RAM and disk, a VPS and shared hosting, or an advertised price and the cost due today. Define technical terms before using them. Explain trade-offs with concrete examples. State uncertainty instead of filling gaps with assumptions.

## Five topic groups

1. **Foundations**: VPS basics, billing terms, hardware, security, and server administration.
2. **Workloads**: n8n, Coolify, WordPress, Docker, Nextcloud, VPNs, and game servers.
3. **Comparisons**: hosting types, providers, locations, prices, and plan trade-offs.
4. **Dataset research**: findings from the reviewed VPS source, normalization method, publication policy, and market snapshots.
5. **Founder and trust notes**: ranking independence, corrections, affiliate disclosures, privacy, and how the service operates.

Every article belongs to at least one group and answers one named reader question. Avoid thin tag pages and keyword-only briefs.

## URL and page conventions

- Index: `/guides`
- Article: `/guides/[slug]`
- Author and standards: `/authors/[slug]` or `/about`
- Feed: `/feed.xml`
- Slugs use lowercase words separated by hyphens and remain stable after publication.
- Draft and preview URLs must not appear in the sitemap or receive indexable metadata.
- Filtered catalog links use the canonical `/catalog` query format and must not create indexable duplicate pages.

## Required article record

Every published article supplies typed frontmatter for:

- `title`, `description`, `slug`, `author`
- `publishedAt`, `updatedAt`, `topics`, `reviewStatus`
- `dataSnapshotAt` when the article uses catalog facts
- `canonicalUrl` when the article is syndicated elsewhere

Guide previews are generated by `src/app/guides/[slug]/opengraph-image.tsx` from the published title, description and author. Do not add a static `heroImage` frontmatter field. The image route supplies social metadata, and article JSON-LD points to that same generated PNG.

The article body includes a named reader question, a clear answer, source notes or method, caveats, visible update history, related guides, an appropriate catalog/provider/offer action, and the affiliate disclosure.

## Source and factual standards

- Cite the provider or first-party source for provider-specific claims.
- Cite the immutable research snapshot and explain the method for dataset claims.
- Use the active published snapshot for offer prices, locations, and availability.
- Show checked or published dates beside time-sensitive claims.
- Keep unknown values as unknown. Do not infer hardware, billing terms, performance, or trust scores.
- Keep material corrections visible in the update history and route readers to `/corrections`.
- Commission, sponsorship, affiliate status, and conversion data never influence editorial conclusions or catalog ranking.

## Disclosure and voice

State near the first catalog action that some links may earn commission and that commission does not change ranking. Use plain, specific language. Prefer short paragraphs, descriptive headings, tables when they clarify a choice, and examples that match the reader’s task. Avoid hype, fake urgency, generic introductions, keyword stuffing, and mass-produced or lightly rewritten AI copy.

## Review and freshness

Before publication, an editor checks the reader question, sources, links, metadata, structured data, disclosure, spelling, and catalog action. Articles with snapshot-dependent facts enter the freshness queue. Recheck those articles every 30 days and whenever the referenced publication changes. Update the `updatedAt` date and update history after a material change. Mark unsupported or obsolete articles for removal instead of preserving stale claims for traffic.

## Release checklist

An article is ready when it has:

- a named author and one clear reader question;
- typed frontmatter and a stable slug;
- sources, method, dates, caveats, and disclosure;
- useful links into the catalog, provider, offer, methodology, or corrections route;
- valid canonical, `BlogPosting`, and `BreadcrumbList` facts;
- a freshness owner and next review date.
