If your ship got hit by a cannonball, you'd actually be one of the lucky ones. In the 1700s, the Navy's round shot would smash through a pirate ship's wall and turn it into a storm of splinters that tore through half the crew. The pirates couldn't afford proper ammo, so they fired back with language, which is a fancy word for literal garbage. But the Navy had a nastier idea called Hot Shot. They shoveled cannonballs into a furnace until they glowed red, carried them over with giant tongs, and dropped them in behind a soaking wet plug so the gunpowder wouldn't go off early. The ball buried itself deep in the pirate ship's hull, and a few minutes later, the whole ship lit up from the inside like a giant lantern. Not to be outdone, the pirates fired back with the carcass, a hollow cannonball packed with burning tar and punched full of holes, so it spat fire the whole way over and turned the entire Navy ship into a torch. Eventually, ships advanced from wood to iron, and the fireballs just bounced off. So, America built the Rodman Gun, a cannon that fired a single ball weighing over a thousand pounds, made to punch a hole in an iron warship big enough to sink it in one shot.
