He saw gaps into a railroad every 39 feet. You see, two companies are racing to build a railroad along a canyon so deep you can't see the bottom. And whoever finishes first gets the whole country's business. He's the youngest engineer on the job. And when his boss sees a finger-wide gap between every single rail, his face goes purple. You're wrecking my railroad! But he won't close a single one. The other company lays their rails end to end, perfectly smooth, and finishes first. Their trains glide across the canyon without a sound, while his go clickety-clack over every gap. The newspapers start calling it the clickety-clack line, and everyone switches to the quiet trains. Then summer comes, and something weird happens. His gaps start shrinking. In May, you can fit a coin in them. By July, barely a sheet of paper, and his boss is thrilled. See? Even your gaps know you were wrong. Meanwhile, on the perfect railroad, the rails have started groaning in the afternoon sun, pushing against each other like they want to get out. On the hottest day anyone can remember, his gaps finally close. Rail touching rail. And halfway across the canyon, the perfect railroad kicks sideways like a snake, right as a train rolls onto it.
