This man is about to die, so someone sent for the bird.
In the 1600s, when plague hit a town, the doctor showed up dressed like a nightmare.
Black robe, black hat, and a long curved beak over his whole face.
It wasn't a costume. It was a gas mask, four hundred years early.
You see, doctors believed plague travelled through the air as a poison fog they called miasma.
Bad air, bad smells, dead people. To them, it was the same thing.
So the beak was stuffed with roses, mint, and camphor.
Every breath he took got filtered through flowers.
The robe was leather, rubbed with wax, sealing him from head to toe.
He carried a long stick, so he could examine patients and lift their clothes without ever touching them.
And the strangest part? It kind of worked.
Not because of the herbs. Herbs did nothing.
But a waxed coat and a beak meant the real killers, flea bites, couldn't reach his skin.
Plague doctors kept dying anyway. Most of them never stood a chance.
But the ones who sealed up tight and kept their distance lived a little longer.
They were fighting the wrong enemy, with almost the right suit.
Four hundred years ago, that was as close to a hazmat suit as you could get.
