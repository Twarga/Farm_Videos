So the state stepped in. Ice became a regulated treasure.
Melt someone else's ice, or let it slip? You could lose your hand.
Some emperors even built ice houses under temples, like vaults for frozen gold.
And here's the crazy part. It worked.
For a few coins you could buy a chipped cup of snow in July.
Rich Romans hosted parties just to prove they had ice, right there in the heat.
The workers got frostbite hauling it down the mountain.
The aristocrats got brain freeze drinking it up.
It took seventeen hundred years before anyone thought, maybe ice shouldn't be illegal to melt.
