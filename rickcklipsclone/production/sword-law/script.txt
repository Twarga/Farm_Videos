This man is being fined, and he hasn't broken a single law.
Well, he has. In medieval England, the law said every free man must own a weapon.
And not just own one. Be trained on it, and keep it ready.
The rule was called the Assize of Arms, and it worked like a tax bracket of violence.
The poorest man owed a gambeson and a spear. The richest? Chain mail and a warhorse.
Officials rode through towns checking, and if you came up short, you paid.
Why? Because the king had no army he could call on demand.
The citizens were the army. Every blacksmith was a waiting soldier.
If invaders landed on the coast, the alarm went out village by village.
Men grabbed whatever they owned and ran to fight for free.
It saved the crown a fortune. A standing army costs a fortune; a legally armed public costs nothing.
The catch? An armed public is also a dangerous public, and kings spent centuries worrying about both.
And here's the wild part. The weapon laws never really died.
England's last archery requirement stayed on the books until the 1960s.
For seven hundred years, the government's backup plan was basically "bring your own army."
